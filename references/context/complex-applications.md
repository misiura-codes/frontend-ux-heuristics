# Complex Applications

Use alongside the H1-H10 helpers when reviewing enterprise tools, domain-specific software, data-analysis products, admin consoles, modeling tools, high-impact decision systems, or nonlinear workflows for trained users.

## Review Lens

- Treat complexity as task/domain reality, not permission for confusing UI.
- Support broad, unstructured goals with visible state, recoverable exploration, and efficient repeated work.
- Assume trained users still rely on general web/platform conventions and still benefit from plain language.
- Prioritize learnability, trust, and efficiency because errors and delays compound across long workflows.
- Do not rely on training or documentation as the primary way users learn or recover in the moment.

## Cross-Heuristic Prompts

- H1: Show detailed progress for long-running tasks: completed steps, current step, elapsed time, remaining work, or decision-useful wait information.
- H2: Use domain terms users actually use, but avoid internal jargon, misleading metaphors, and icons that conflict with real-world conventions.
- H3: Preserve work through version history, autosave, drafts, restore points, undo, cancel, and backtracking across long sessions.
- H4: Maintain internal consistency across modules and external consistency with web, platform, and industry conventions.
- H5: Let users safely explore by previewing parameter changes, detecting risky combinations, and showing effects before commit.
- H6: Provide recognition cues for dense domain objects: labels, images, definitions, hover details, examples, and visible prior choices.
- H7: Provide accelerators for expert plateau: shortcuts, macros, gestures, templates, saved views, bulk actions, and customization.
- H8: Use staged disclosure to reduce visual-search load; remove redundant icons, gratuitous graphics, and low-value decorative data.
- H9: Make errors descriptive and constructive; link to deeper docs only after giving direct recovery guidance.
- H10: Provide brief in-context help for commands, concepts, shortcuts, and rare actions; keep robust docs searchable for deeper troubleshooting.

## Evidence

Look for progress step lists, preview panes, history/timeline restore, saved views, macros, domain-object labels, contextual command help, searchable docs, staged disclosure, consistent module actions, and error messages with direct recovery and documentation links.

## Failure Patterns

- Generic spinners hide long-running system work.
- Frequent users still hesitate because icons, labels, or terms conflict with domain expectations.
- Users lose high-effort work while experimenting or learning by doing.
- Dense screens show every option at once instead of staging advanced or rare controls.
- Expert workflows plateau because repeated work lacks accelerators or customization.
- Errors assume users know system-model details, codes, administrators, or documentation paths.
- Training is expected to compensate for missing in-context guidance.
