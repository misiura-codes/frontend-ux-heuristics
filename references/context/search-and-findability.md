# Search and Findability

Use alongside H2, H6, H9, and H10 when reviewing site search, app search, documentation search, command search, search suggestions, scoped search, filters, no-results states, or large information spaces.

## Search Entry

- Make search visible where users expect it, especially on home, documentation, support, and complex interior pages.
- Prefer a type-in search field over a hidden search link for complex sites and help centers.
- Keep the input wide enough for typical queries and label it plainly.
- Support short, simple queries; do not rely on advanced syntax or Boolean operators.
- Avoid multiple search boxes on one page unless their scopes are unmistakable.

## Results and Refinement

- Optimize first-query success; many users abandon after poor first results.
- Put the most likely answers on the first results page.
- Highlight matched terms and provide enough snippet context to judge relevance.
- Offer spelling correction, synonyms, suggestions, recent queries, and related terms when helpful.
- Treat filters and facets as visible refinement tools, not hidden requirements for basic success.
- Preserve query, filters, and scope as users move between results and detail pages.

## Scoped Search

- Default to all content unless the context clearly calls for a narrow scope.
- Show the active scope on the input and results page.
- Provide one-click expansion to all content when scoped results fail.
- Suggest narrowing when results are too broad and broadening when results are empty.
- Do not let scoped search silently hide relevant help, products, users, or records.

## No Results and Help Search

- Say what was searched and why nothing matched.
- Offer recovery: clear filters, broaden scope, check spelling, try suggested terms, browse categories, or contact support.
- In docs/help, categorize topics and highlight common or recommended content.
- Link errors, empty states, and setup blockers directly to the relevant help article or search result.
- Make search-result pages scannable through headings, snippets, highlighted terms, and stable metadata.

## Evidence

Look for visible search field, plain label, adequate input width, relevant first result, query suggestions, active scope, clear filters, preserved query state, no-results recovery, categorized docs, and direct links from errors to relevant help.

## Failure Patterns

- Search is hidden behind an icon or link on a complex page.
- Advanced search is pushed before simple search has failed.
- Users search the wrong scope because multiple boxes or filters are ambiguous.
- Results require repeated reformulation before useful answers appear.
- No-results pages dead-end without suggestions or broader scope.
- Documentation search returns marketing pages instead of task-focused help.
